# Natural Healing

**By Frank Nadvorník, Naturopath**  
Box 333, Dickinson, N. D.

---

Man, as we read in the Bible, is created from clay, from the earth, and therefore, being a part of nature, he certainly belongs to it and is subject to it. From this it follows that if he departs from nature and does not obey its laws, and if he lives incorrectly, he commits a sin against his own nature, and this sin is then punished by nature itself. Man must therefore return to nature and seek in it the true remedy.

Natural healing teaches that only nature is able to cure diseases, and that this healing power is placed by nature in man himself, and it is up to us only not to hinder it in its progress, to leave it free passage in its development, to support this healing power and to fully trust in it. For example, fever we must quench with cold water, or bring the body to a sweat so that as many spoiled substances as possible are sweated out. If we did not do this, then the inner strength would succumb and the body would “burn up.” It is similar with all other diseases.

For this self-help bodily strength we have many different means by which we enable nature to work more easily by drawing blood away from the inflamed place, from the head and chest downward, or again we warm a chilly body, open clogged pores, cleanse the blood and set it into better circulation, etc., and this happens only when we keep the skin clean, breathe pure air and eat non-irritating, nutritious food. There can be no talk here of any medicine. It is pure nonsense that a bottle with some more or less poisonous liquid could cure a disease: only nature itself can do that, and as Bilz says, the best medicine is not to use medicines. And if we still want to use something, we brew a tea from medicinal herbs and know that we have pure goods that will not harm us.

Our main healing agents are: the sun, air, water, earth, medicinal herbs, and a sensible way of life. With these things we manipulate in every possible way, whereby the disease is best and most naturally removed from the body, and a person remains healthy if he continues to live in accordance with the laws of nature. Only with appetite and confidence throw yourselves into the healing and persevere in it! I am already convinced in advance that you will never regret it. I myself tested this healing on myself, for I too suffered, suffered very much, and when I was almost already dying, and completely abandoned by doctors and medicine and recognized as lost (yes, one physician offered his entire fortune for my life), this healing cured me (then the aforementioned physician told me that a miracle had happened to me).

**Rules** which you must follow exactly during healing are: — Above all, proper cleanliness in everything. The most important of all things is that before using cold and icy water the body must be properly warm, that is, we must not feel chilly in the body, but we also must not be overheated, when the blood in the body is literally foaming. If we are not moderately warm before taking an application, let us first warm ourselves by rubbing with a coarse cloth. — In the room it must be moderately warm and the windows closed when using the means, but if we lie in a compress or wrap, the window may be slightly open. — Compresses and wraps after use must be properly washed in warm and in cold water and hung with the blankets in the sun or frost. — Everything must be done quickly, without delays, skillfully, but not hastily, but precisely, gracefully, not carelessly. — After every cold and icy application there must follow warming either by movement, rubbing or covering in bed. — In the bedroom the window must be open all night, best the upper sash (open it more or less according to the weather and wind). The sick person must have fresh air day and night. As much as possible he must move outside and breathe deeply, do light work or walk. — Put all worry and trouble aside and think only of the cure and fully trust in it, knowing that in yourself is the greatest healing power which will cure you if you support it in a natural way. — Women during menstruation should not use any of these means.

**Diet.** Food occupies great importance both in illness and in health, therefore we must devote proper attention to it. It does not depend on how much we eat, but on how much we digest. To this Dr. Batěk remarks that we should chew every bite so long until we get all the taste out of it and until it is properly mixed with saliva, only then can it be well digested.

The ordinary meat — potato — white bread — coffee — sugar — and — fable diet is absolutely bad food, for it creates a morbid condition in the body and does not contain enough alkaline mineral elements on which the cleansing of the system from morbid and poisonous substances in the body depends. Generally, however, a vegetarian diet is praised, vegetables and fruit containing plenty of nutritious salts so very necessary for our organism. Therefore arrange for yourself a plant diet and bread from wheat and rye groats, that is properly ground into flour; such bread is the most nutritious and helps against constipation. But never eat much, and chew every bite at least twenty times. Breakfast only light, some vegetables, fruit, orange, grapes, a cup of malt coffee with milk. For lunch a plate of vegetable soup, a slice of whole-rye or Graham bread, sometimes 1 soft-boiled egg, sometimes a baked potato, banana or some nuts, 5–10 dried cooked prunes for easy stool, salad and other vegetables, fruit. For supper a plate of bread soup. Dry black bread hard, crush it into coarse dust, boil water or milk, pour in about 2 spoons and let it boil 2 minutes, salt a little and grease with butter. Vegetables, fruit and some spinach, a cup of malt coffee. Further one can prepare various nutritious, non-irritating and meatless dishes. Coffee, tea, sweets, fine pastries, spices and much salt — all that is worthless.

**Lung Gymnastics.** Every day before breakfast and before sleeping in pure, fresh air it is done thus: Exhale all breath. Now inhale slowly through the nose and at the same time slowly raise the arms upward, incline the head backward, draw in as much breath as possible, hold it, clasp the hands and place them behind the head, and incline this forward, expand the chest as much as possible, place the hands behind, and now slowly exhale, lower the arms, let them hang freely and exhale all breath. This is repeated at first seven times and after about a week fourteen times. If you do not want to raise the arms so that someone does not look at you that you are doing a “comedy” (foolish prejudice), place them on the hips, and on inhalation expand the chest properly and on exhalation compress it. After this gymnastics you will feel that you have drawn into yourself a new source of strength, the breath of life. You can also do this gymnastics at an open window.

**Air Bath.** Every day in the forenoon and afternoon, always at least for 10–15 minutes, undress at an open window, let the air blow on you, at the same time make various movements, and with bare hands rub yourself all over. In the country and in summer this bath can be taken outside.

**Sun Bath.** “Where the sun shines, there is no need of a doctor.” That is an old and certainly true proverb. But sun baths are used with great success only in natural-healing institutes or in the country; in the house only by lying on a sofa at a large window into which the sun shines, we let the body be irradiated from the front and from the back (not the head), and when we have warmed ourselves well (baked) and sweated, we wash ourselves with cold water, first the nape, shoulder blades, chest, back, thighs and legs. It is better to wrap oneself in a light cover than to be completely naked, because we sweat sooner and more easily.

**Natural Bath.** Nothing acts so healingly as this bath, in which man is closest to nature, using its most powerful elements simultaneously: sun, air, water and earth. Go to a river, to a brook or somewhere behind a hill out of sight and take a little water with you, there undress, make various movements, gymnastics, various games, lung gymnastics, etc., briefly bathe or wash yourself and let yourself be fanned by the sun’s rays and air (leave a straw hat on the head). This bath can last whole hours or even half a day, and that it has a great and healing effect is attested by the fact that after it we feel as if newly born.

**Clay Ointment (from clay).** We leave a clod of clay to soften in water, pour off the water and from the remaining mass remove all little stones and dirt. Then we crush the clay with a piece of wood, add a little vinegar or a decoction of tormentil, plantain or horsetail and mix well. This ointment we spread on a cloth and apply to every painful place or ulcer, lupus, cancer, swelling, sprain, inflammation, etc.

**Hot Compress (Napárka).** We wring well a double-folded piece of flannel soaked in hot water and apply it to the painful part of the body as hot as can be borne, and renew it every 5 minutes until the pain yields. This hot compress helps against pains in the intestines, colic, indigestion, constipation, in cramps, nerve pains, toothache, facial neuralgia, etc.

**Massage.** This is performed as a strengthening means for the whole body beginning with the remote parts, first the legs and arms, then the nape and back, the small of the back and finally the chest and abdomen, and consists mainly in gentle and gradually increasing stroking, rubbing, kneading and tapping with the fingers, palms and the flat of the hand, and the whole massage lasts 25–40 minutes. During stroking and rubbing the body is anointed with vaseline or olive oil; kneading and tapping is done with dry hands. We always stroke and rub toward the center of the body, from the hands upward to the shoulders, from the shoulders to the waist, and from the legs upward to the waist. Abdominal massage is performed with the flat of the hand in circles to the right about 25 circles, and is used against abdominal diseases and against constipation. Further local massage for swellings, neuralgia, muscular rheumatism, sciatica, pain in the small of the back, etc., consists in arched stroking with the fingers of one hand with moderate pressure, and the other hand rubs in the direction from the swelling, and lasts about 10 minutes. — Kneading is done as if we were kneading and pulling dough; also with three fingers we pinch the skin; tapping with the palms set upright, the fingers being spread. The masseur must work through the body playfully but emphatically, and must leave a pleasant feeling. At the beginning gentle stroking and rubbing, then more strongly acting kneading and tapping. After the whole-body massage a bath in moderately lukewarm water or washing and rubbing with water is used. Also a steam bath for 25 minutes or a bed steam bath can be used, whereby the skin becomes finer, more blood-filled and the vessels and lymphatics expand.

**Washing (Omývání).** Every morning, as soon as we get up, we dip a towel in cold water, wring it so that water does not drip from it, and wash both legs and soles, calves, thighs and hips, arms, nape and back, chest and abdomen, and we can lie down in a warmed bed for a while, or dress and move so that we warm ourselves. Washing in bed for weak persons is done in the same way, only after washing each part is immediately covered so as to preserve as much heat as possible. The whole washing lasts about 1 minute and the body must beforehand be warm.

**Treading in Water.** Into a larger vessel we pour about 5 inches of cold water, step into it and continually tread and rub one foot against the other; this lasts ¼, ½, 1 to 2 minutes according to what is pleasant to each. After stepping out of the water we dry the feet well and walk barefoot about the room until we feel pleasant warmth in the feet, or after stepping out of the water we lie down in bed and wrap the feet well without drying so that they warm as soon as possible. This simple means can always be taken when the feet are warm, and acts favorably in that it draws blood into the feet and lightens the upper body, acts on the kidneys and carries off urine and gases from the stomach, facilitates breathing and relieves headache. Whoever treads water before sleeping soon falls asleep and sleeps well.

**Warm Foot Bath.** Into a larger vessel (tin tub) we pour about 6–8 inches of water 95 degrees F. warm, into which we throw a handful of salt, or several handfuls of scalded hay chaff or oat straw, and bathe the feet in it for 15 minutes; then we dip the feet for a moment into cold water, dry them well and walk barefoot about the room. After a salt bath the feet are not dipped in cold water. — This is used with great benefit for the anemic, nervous, in circulatory disorders, diseases of the head and neck, blood rushes, cramps and in the absence of menstruation; also for sweating feet, open wounds, swelling, gout, ulcers, bruises and injuries of all kinds, likewise for arthritis, corns and rheumatism, and is best taken in the evening.

**Hot Foot Bath.** The sick person undresses and sits on the bed. The attendant wraps him well in two blankets and a cover, fastens it around the neck, and wraps the feet up to under the calves. Now the sick person puts his feet into water as hot as he can bear, about to the middle of the calves. After a while hot water is poured in, by inserting the hand into the water and pouring from a teapot onto the palm and immediately stirring so that it does not scald the patient’s feet. As soon as the sick person has sweated properly, which lasts about 15 minutes, we lay him in that wrapping, and wrap the feet well so that he sweats still longer 15–20 minutes in bed; then we wash him all over with cold water and cover him only lightly for a while. — It is recommended for those who suffer from over-irritation of the nerves or weakness, and against chronically cold feet, but in asthmatic attacks it helps excellently; after a short while we feel relief in the chest and the breath becomes free.

**Warm Sitz Bath.** Into a larger tub we pour about 4 buckets of water 90 degrees F. warm. First, however, we boil for 5 minutes a handful of horsetail, or oat straw, or hay chaff, and pour it into the water. Now we sit in the tub so that the kidneys are also in the water (we lean a little backward) and leave the feet outside, which we wrap, and thus we sit 15 minutes. — This bath serves, namely with horsetail, for cramps, rheumatic pains of the bladder and kidneys, bladder stone and difficulties in urination; with oat straw for dry breaking and gout; with hay chaff for difficulties in the abdomen, swellings, ulcers, fissures, hard stool, hemorrhoids and other difficulties in the anus and wind colic. One suffering from hernia need not fear this bath, but women, shortly before, after and during menstruation, should not take it. After the bath the bathed part is wiped with a towel wrung out in cold water.

**Friction Sitz Bath.** We pour into a tub about 5 inches of cold water, sit in it (leave the feet outside) and with both hands throw water onto the abdomen and at the same time rub the stomach, abdomen, lower abdomen and sexual organs up to the anus. This lasts according to circumstances 1 to 5 minutes. Then we step into the tub and quickly wash the feet, thighs, arms, nape, neck, chest and back and immediately rub ourselves all over with a brush (with a bent handle so that we can reach the back) and then still with bare hands until we are completely dry and feel warm. Women during menstruation should not take this bath. — Excellent successes are achieved with this bath in poor digestion and difficulties in the abdomen, and mainly strengthening of the nerves, because precisely in the places of the generative and reproductive organs so many ends of the most important nerves, namely the main abdominal nerves, converge and by their connection with the brain and spinal cord a favorable effect is produced on the entire nervous system. This bath can also be taken in a tub, and then the feet are also in the water, but we raise the knees. In favorable weather the window in the bathroom may always be open. We can also rub ourselves dry with the hands for warming before we step into the water.

**Upper Steam Bath.** The patient bares the upper part of the body to the waist, ties a dry towel around himself. He sits on a chair and another is placed near it and he supports himself with his hands on it. On this chair a pot of boiling water is placed, and this whole apparatus together with the patient is completely covered and wrapped in blankets; the sick person sits with his head inclined over the steam, having eyes and mouth open, and inhales the steam through nose and mouth. This steam bath lasts 15 to 25 minutes, after which follows a strong washing of the upper part of the body with cold or icy water, and the sick person dresses without drying and moves in a warm room or lies down in bed; he must not go out for an hour. Into the boiling water we throw a spoon of ground fennel, which strengthens and cleanses the eyes and respiratory organs. Also useful: sage, yarrow, mint, elder, plantain and linden blossom. — The effects of this bath are considerable; it opens the pores, dissolves and removes impure substances from the nose, windpipe and lungs; it acts well in colds, headache, ringing in the ears, rheumatic and crampy pains in the neck and shoulders and all accompanying catarrhs. Also in swellings of the head, eruptions on the head, discharge from the ears and blood rushes to the head. Usually this bath is prescribed twice a week, but moderation is desirable.

**Lower Steam Bath.** The patient bares the lower part of the body to the waist, sits on a chair, to which a pot of boiling water is placed, over which a narrow board is laid on which the sick person places his feet. Now he is covered to the middle of the body together with the chair, and lets the steam stream upward over the feet. Into the boiling water we throw a handful of hay chaff. This bath lasts 20 to 30 minutes, after which follows a quick washing, either only of the steamed parts, and if the whole body sweated then the whole body is washed with cold or icy water, and without drying the patient dresses and moves about the room. — This bath is used for many foot diseases: for swollen feet, if the feet sweat immoderately, for cold feet, for ulcers and unsuccessful operations on corns where blood poisoning threatens; for all diseases of the lower abdomen that arose from catching cold, and also for blood rushes to the head. Moderation is desirable here, however.

**Full Steam Bath.** This we best procure by buying a so-called Vapor Bath Cabinet, as offered in catalogs, but this full steam bath is very rarely recommended, because its more frequent use weakens the sick person too much; therefore partial baths are preferred, and mainly mild bed baths.

**Bed Steam Bath.** We spread a cover on the bed and on it two blankets. We fill a gallon jug with hot water and wrap it with a cloth soaked in hot water and well wrung out. Now we take a large sheet (so that it reaches from the armpits over the feet), soak it in cold or lukewarm water and wring it and spread the double-folded one on those blankets, on which the sick person now lies, and we wrap him in that sheet, place the jug with hot water under his feet, then wrap him together with the jug in the blankets and cover (he places his hands on the sheet and blankets, but under the cover which reaches to the neck). Everything is well tucked around the feet, and around the neck still a piece of cloth is given, and tucked behind the shoulders. We can also place at the knees on each side one bottle filled with hot water and wrapped with a cloth wrung out in hot water. If the hands are cold, we use two more bottles which we place at the sides of the trunk, and the sick person places his hands on them. We cork the jug and bottles well, but do not fill them up to the corks. Clay bottles are better, because there is no fear that they will burst, whereas glass bottles filled with hot water may burst and scald the sick person. We can still cover the sick person with one more cover or featherbed. This bath lasts 1 to 2 hours, above all as long as it is pleasant to the sick person. As soon as he begins to feel unwell, we immediately interrupt the bath. After that follows washing or wiping of the whole body with cold or quite icy water in bed, and we still cover the sick person lightly for a short time so that he warms up. This bed steam bath is better than other steam baths, because it does not cause discomfort and acts so gently that it can be used both for infants and for old people without hesitation. (With this single bath I once saved a child 1 year old which already had the left side of the lungs completely inflamed). — It is recommended especially in chronic disease states where it is necessary to bring the nerves into better activity and to irritate the slack, unclean skin so that thereby morbid substances are loosened and eliminated, and so that the internal organs are lightened, and at the same time so that the sick person does not weaken. In many chronic states, such as gout, tendency to rheumatism, syphilis, etc., which require longer treatment, this bath is used about 2 to 3 times a week. — Note. In fever, heart defects, lung diseases, steam baths should not be used without expert advice; above all in severe illnesses we must not force sweat by using a steam bath. Also in severe nervous diseases, in anemia and chlorosis steam baths are less recommended.

**Compress on the Stomach.** A piece of coarse linen (soft burlap) we soak in lukewarm, cold or icy water (we can pour a little vinegar into it), wring it well, fold it three to four times and apply it to the stomach from the chest about to the middle of the abdomen and about to the middle of the body on the sides, then we apply a several-fold blanket which must overlap the wet compress on all sides, and we tie everything around the back. We leave it on the body, according to circumstances, 2 hours or the whole night. — This compress aids digestion and removes gases from the stomach, flatulence, and acts beneficially in stomach diseases. Wringed in hot water and applied it soothes colic and cramps in the stomach, but then it is always wrung again in hot water every 15 minutes and reapplied.

**Hot Compress on the Chest and Abdomen.** Coarse linen is soaked in hot water, wrung, folded four times and applied to the chest and abdomen as hot as can be borne; over it a double-folded blanket is placed, but larger so that it overlaps the wet compress everywhere. — This hot compress performs good service in asthmatic attacks, soothing the cramps. Always after 15 minutes it is wrung in hot water and reapplied, and this is repeated several times until the attack yields.

**Body Wrap (Ovin).** We spread on the bed a 2- to 4-fold folded blanket, on it we place 2- to 4-fold folded coarse linen wrung in lukewarm, cold or icy water. On this sheet we lie down and from both sides we throw it over ourselves and pull it tight, then we throw the blanket from both sides, pull it tight and pin it so that everything fits closely to the body. The blanket must overlap the wet wrap above and below so that air does not penetrate to the body. The wrap is so large that it begins under the breasts and ends under the lower abdomen, but it can also be wider so that it reaches up under the armpits, and then the chest is also wrapped. — This wrap, applied at night, performs incredible service in the most various diseases and illnesses. Thus against cough, cold, blood rush to the head, headache, toothache, in loss of appetite, etc.
